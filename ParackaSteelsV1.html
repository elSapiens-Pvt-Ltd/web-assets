Current System & Challenges

How the business is operating today — and where it is breaking down

Two Shops, Two Billing Systems — Not Synced
A single business operating as two disconnected entities
Core Problem
The Situation
The steel shop and the hardware shop each run their own billing system. To the outside world it is one business — Paracka Traders — but internally the two systems do not talk to each other. A customer who buys cement from the steel shop and paint from the hardware shop exists as two separate entities across the two systems.
What They Did to Work Around It
  • Set up a VPN so both shops can access a shared network
  • Customer accounts are maintained in the hardware shop system as the single source of truth for account relationships
  • Each customer is created twice — once in the steel shop system and once in the hardware shop system, under different account heads
  • The customer accounts module holds the relationship between the two entries, manually linking the same person across both shops — transactions from both shops are also recorded here
  • From the customer accounts module, staff can view year-wise aging — outstanding balances broken down by age across both shops for a given customer
⚠️ The Consequence — Duplicate Entry, Human Error
  • Each shop creates its own customer head independently — the steel shop staff create one, the hardware shop staff create another, for the same person
  • To establish the relationship between these two customer heads, a person in the hardware shop manually types a customer accounts entry — there is no data export or import, no automation whatsoever
  • This is entirely dependent on one person doing it correctly every time — any mistake in linking creates a data mismatch that is hard to detect and correct
  • If that person is on leave, the linking simply does not happen — customer relationships go unrecorded and the data gap accumulates
Software Challenges
The two core obstacles to any digital implementation
1 · Staff Adaptability
The workforce has operated with traditional processes for decades. Introducing new software requires not just training but a genuine shift in daily habits — from paper to screen, from memory to system. Resistance to change, low digital confidence, and the fear of disrupting a workflow that has worked for years are all real factors.
  • Field staff are not always desk-based — any tool must work on a mobile phone
  • The learning curve must be minimal; complex interfaces will not be adopted
  • Parallel running of books and software will continue during the transition period
2 · Data Unorganised
The existing data across the two billing systems is not in a clean or structured state. Duplicate customer entries, missing phone numbers, year-wise aging reports instead of actionable buckets, and manual account linking have left the data in a condition where it cannot simply be imported and used — it requires cleaning, reconciliation, and verification before it is reliable.
  • Phone numbers missing — not mandatory in current system
  • Aging report format (year-wise) not suited for collection use
  • Account relationships maintained manually — prone to human error

Tradition + Innovation

Old-style books still run alongside the digital system

Tradition + Innovation
Old-style books still run alongside the digital system
This is a business that has earned trust over four decades. That longevity comes with deep-rooted processes — many of which are still paper-based. Traditional account books continue to be maintained even as digital systems are in place. Both systems run in parallel.
Design implication: Any digital solution must respect this reality. The transition cannot be abrupt. The system needs to be intuitive enough for people who are used to ledger books, and must earn trust gradually — not replace everything at once.
  • Staff already use Invaccs accounting software alongside physical ledgers — they are not new to digital tools, but any change to an established workflow brings its own resistance and requires careful transition
  • Paper records serve as a fallback and verification layer — this should be respected, not forced out
  • Any new system must have a low learning curve and must not disrupt the daily rhythm of the shop floor

Receivables Categorisation

How existing outstanding data will be classified to drive the collection process

Three-Tier Classification
All outstanding trades will be assigned one of three categories based on date and activity
1
Pending
Up to March 31, 2026 — Not Active After March 31, 2026
Trades that were active up to the March 31, 2026 cut-off date but have had no activity since. These are overdue accounts where payment has stalled. Priority collection targets.
2
Running
After March 31, 2026 — Active
Current active trades with transactions after March 31, 2026. These are ongoing business relationships with outstanding balances that are part of the normal trade cycle. Monitored for timely collection within the 1-month rotation target.
3
Long Pending
Before March 31, 2025 — Still Active
Trades with outstanding dues dating before March 31, 2025, where the account remains active. These are the oldest receivables — over a year outstanding — and represent the highest-risk portion of the ₹3.5 Cr. Requires immediate escalation and structured follow-up.

Stock Management

Why conventional stock management cannot be applied here

Scheme-Driven Procurement — Paints
Hardware shop · Paint companies
Constraint
How It Works
Paint companies periodically offer schemes — discounted pricing tied to a minimum purchase quantity. To access the scheme price, the dealer must buy the quantity the company specifies during that scheme window. If the required quantity is not purchased, the scheme price is forfeited and the dealer pays the regular rate.
⚠️ Why Standard Stock Management Does Not Apply
  • Purchase quantity is dictated by the supplier's scheme, not by actual sales demand or ideal stock levels
  • Buying less than the scheme quantity means losing the low price — which directly erodes the already thin margin on paints
  • This means stock can be bought in volumes higher than current demand simply to lock in the scheme rate
  • Reorder-point logic or demand-based purchasing models cannot override this supplier-driven buying pattern
🚫 Not Handled — Out of Current Scope
The following are identified as future needs but are not being addressed in the current engagement:
  • Scheme-triggered purchase orders — where the trigger is a supplier scheme, not a low-stock alert
  • Recording scheme details (scheme price, required quantity, validity window) against a purchase
  • Stock reporting that distinguishes intentional scheme-driven stock from genuine overstocking

Requirements

Functional requirements, process decisions, and operational needs

Phase 1 — Collection System Implementation
1 · What Is the Requirement
📌 Requirement
Streamline the Collection Process

The primary objective of this engagement is to bring structure and visibility to the receivables collection process. With ₹3.5 Crore outstanding and no systematic follow-up in place, the business needs a clear system to track, prioritise, and act on outstanding dues.

2 · How Is It Currently Done
📝 Current Process
Bill-Level Collection Assignment

When a bill is created, the system offers two optional fields:

  • Collection Route — a carryover from the traditional system where a person physically covered a specific area on a set day (Monday route, Tuesday route, etc.). When the business moved digital, this structure was copied across as-is
  • Collection Rep — the person responsible for collecting against this bill

Neither field is mandatory. If left empty, the bill has no owner — it simply exists as an outstanding amount with no one accountable for it.

📝 Current Outstanding Communication
How Balances Are Currently Sent to Customers

Outstanding balance statements are sent to customers from the customer accounts module. The statement shows:

  • Total pending — the combined outstanding balance
  • Split into Hardware and Steel — so the customer can understand which shop the dues belong to

This split view must be preserved in the new system — customers are used to seeing it this way and it aids reconciliation on their end.

3 · Challenges in Streamlining It
⚠️ Challenge 1 — Data Reliability
Current Customer Data Is 70–80% Reliable — Not Migration-Ready As-Is

The existing customer data is approximately 70–80% reliable. The same customer exists under different account heads across the steel and hardware billing systems, linked manually through the customer accounts module — and human errors have accumulated throughout. The data cannot simply be imported and used without cleaning and verification.

Additionally, phone numbers are not mandatory in the current system, meaning a significant portion of customer records have no contact information — making collection calls impossible without external lookup.

⚠️ Challenge 2 — Aging Report Format
Bill Aging Report is Year-Wise — Not Useful for Collection

The current billing system generates the aging report broken down by year. For collection purposes, this is not actionable — a year-wide bucket gives no granularity on how old a specific bill is within that year, making it impossible to prioritise which customer to call first or identify bills approaching critical delay thresholds.

⚠️ Challenge 3 — No Visibility on Outstanding Dues
All Receivables Sit in the Same Unclassified Pool

Currently, outstanding data is not categorised. All receivables — whether 2 weeks old or 2 years old, from active customers or dormant ones — sit in the same pool. This makes it impossible to prioritise collection efforts, identify risk, or hold anyone accountable for follow-up.

⚠️ Challenge 4 — No Follow-Up Scheduling
Callbacks Are Not Tracked — They Depend on Memory

Traditionally, collection was done by route — one person covering a specific area on Monday, another area on Tuesday, and so on. When the business moved to a digital system, this route structure was carried over as-is. The concept made sense in a physical world where someone was literally travelling a road; in a digital system it adds no value and only creates rigidity.

The deeper problem is that with nearly 500 customers to follow up with in a week, there is no mechanism to schedule a callback for a specific date. When a customer says "call me on Wednesday", the rep has to remember it themselves — and with that volume, callbacks are regularly missed. There is no system surfacing what needs to happen today.

⚠️ Challenge 5 — Sales Rep vs Collection Rep Conflict
Rapport Can Work Against Collection

The person who handled the sale is often the same person making the collection call. The customer has a personal rapport with the sales rep — they feel comfortable pushing back or asking for more time, and the sales rep is less likely to be firm about deadlines. Result: payment dates get pushed repeatedly without consequence.

4 · How We Solve the Challenges
⚠️ Immediate Action — Before Migration
Make Phone Number Mandatory — Now

Before any data export or migration happens, phone number must be made a mandatory field in the existing billing systems (both hardware and steel). Without phone numbers, the collection system cannot function — there is no way to call or WhatsApp the customer. This change must be made now, in parallel with the system build, so data quality improves before the migration point.

✅ Solving Challenge 1 — Data Migration Plan
Export → Import → Link
  • Step 1 — Export the aging report from both the hardware and steel billing systems
  • Step 2 — Export customer account data to extract the existing relationship mappings between the customer heads in each shop
  • Step 3 — Import both datasets into the new web-based system
  • Step 4 — The web system will provide an option to manually link accounts — so the steel-side and hardware-side records for the same customer can be merged under a single identity

Route and collection representative assignments from the current system will not be carried over — these will be set fresh in the new system.

✅ Solving Challenges 2 & 3 — Data Categorisation
Three-Tier Classification + Meaningful Aging Buckets

All existing outstanding data will be classified into three categories based on transaction date and account activity. The new system will present aging in meaningful time buckets (0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 91–180, 181–365, 365+ days) so the collection team can immediately see what is urgent and what is long overdue.

  • Pending — Active up to March 31, 2026; no activity after. Priority collection targets.
  • Running — Active after March 31, 2026. Current trades within the normal business cycle.
  • Long Pending — Dues dating before March 31, 2025. Oldest and highest-risk. Requires immediate escalation.
✅ Solving Challenges 4 & 5 — Structured Follow-Up & Role Separation
Every Outstanding Balance Gets a Clear Next State
  • Scheduled — a follow-up date is set; call or message goes out on that date automatically
  • Deferred by owner — customer has spoken directly with owner; follow-up paused until agreed date
  • Promised payment — customer has committed to a payment date; system monitors and triggers if payment does not arrive
  • Callback requested — customer asked to be called on a specific day; system surfaces it on that day
  • Unresponsive — repeated attempts with no response; escalation flag raised
  • Collected — payment received; record closed

Collection rep and sales rep will be distinct roles — the person who sold is not the person who collects, removing the rapport dynamic that delays payment.

⚠️ Edge Case — Customer Calls the Owner Directly
Owner-Level Conversations Must Suppress System Follow-Up

Sometimes a customer bypasses the collection rep and calls the owner directly to request more time. A system-generated follow-up going out shortly after signals poor internal coordination and damages trust.

The system must allow the owner to log this and defer the follow-up. When deferred: no automated reminder or call goes out, the reason and new expected payment date are recorded, the collection rep is notified, and on the deferred date the follow-up resumes automatically if payment has not been received.

5 · The Solution
✅ The Collection System — elCRM
How the New System Will Work

The collection system will be built within elCRM. Key capabilities:

  • Calling option built into the system — reps can log calls, outcomes, and next actions directly
  • Mobile-friendly — the system must work fully on a phone; field staff are not always desk-based
  • WhatsApp reminders on the 15th and 30th of each month — sent to customers with outstanding balances
    • Auto-scheduled by default
    • Can be triggered manually on demand
    • Can be altered or suppressed before sending
  • Scheduled callbacks — if a customer says "call me Wednesday", the rep logs it and it surfaces automatically on that date
  • Collection rep ≠ sales rep — the system distinguishes between who made the sale and who is responsible for collecting
  • Mandatory rep assignment — every bill must have a collection rep assigned; without an owner, no follow-up happens
  • Call log / interaction history — every contact attempt, outcome, and next action is recorded so nothing falls through
📝 Key Principle
Follow-Up Is Everything

The entire value of the collection system rests on follow-up. A collection call made once and not tracked is worthless. The system must ensure that every outstanding balance has a next action, every next action has a date, and every date is surfaced automatically so nothing falls through. Without structured follow-up, money stays outside.

💡 Opportunity Identified
Customers Don't Know the Hardware Shop Exists

Customers who buy from the steel shop are often unaware that Paracka also operates a hardware shop selling paints, plumbing supplies, and more. The collection touchpoints — calls and WhatsApp messages — are an opportunity to surface the hardware shop to steel customers and vice versa. This can be incorporated into the communication templates.
