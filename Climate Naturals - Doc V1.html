Ungraded Stock Reduction Logic
  • Purchase entry → Stock Receipt — bulk purchase recorded; a batch is created in elInventory; sampling determines the expected qty per grade (e.g., 1000 kg green cardamom → 8 mm: 200 kg, 7-7.5 mm: 600 kg, 5-6 mm: 200 kg)
  • Before (current logic) — ungraded "qty to come" per grade was proportion-based on physically unprocessed qty: Expected proportion × Remaining unprocessed qty. If 500 kg processed → 8 mm ungraded shows 100 kg (20 % × 500 kg), regardless of actual output
  • New logic — ungraded starts at the full expected qty from sampling and reduces by actual output received:
    Ungraded per grade = max(0, Expected qty − Actual output received so far)
  • Got exactly or more than expected — ungraded hits 0 and stays 0; any extra output still adds to grade stock normally
  • Got less than expected, batch still in progress — shows the remaining gap (e.g., expected 200 kg 8 mm, got 150 kg → ungraded still shows 50 kg for 8 mm)
  • Batch grading marked complete — all remaining ungraded goes to 0 regardless of actual vs expected variance
  • Sampling Report — full expected vs actual reconciliation (qty and %) available in the Sampling report
  • Warehouse / grading team — processing grading runs
  • Inventory managers — monitoring ungraded stock
  • Operations managers — tracking qty available per grade
 Feature Update
Grading team records runs as before; ungraded qty per grade now reduces by actual output received — behaviour of the ungraded view changes from proportion-based to output-based reduction 		Low

Order Creation Switches to elPOS

Migrating ensures all historical account records are available inside elAccounts elAccounts is also a dependency for the accounting system (elBooks)
  • After migration, both databases will remain in sync — any record in elAccounts is reflected in the Climate Naturals db and vice versa

elPOS
Roles & Permissions
  • Salesperson — sell, take payment, add/edit customers, apply manual discount, raise void requests (cancellation requests), view orders & reprint
  • Store Manager — all salesperson actions + approve voids (order cancellations), issue refunds, manage catalog/loyalty/surcharges, configure store settings, terminals, printers, and payment providers; access all reports and sync screens
Cashier Session
  • Select Store → Select Terminal → Open Session (opening cash float recorded)
  • Close Session with duration summary and optional handover notes
Cart & Checkout
  • Billing screen — category tabs, a product grid, and search; the cashier taps an item to add it to the cart
  • The cart — added items sit here with a quantity stepper so the cashier can adjust counts or remove a line; price + tax recompute as they go
  • Discounts — the cashier can type a manual discount (either a percentage or a fixed rupee amount) with a mandatory reason for the audit trail. If the customer is on a loyalty plan, that discount is applied automatically — no extra click needed
  • Extra charges — the cashier picks from a saved list of charges (e.g. Service Charge, Rush Fee) or types a one-off; each charge prints as its own line on the bill
  • Freight — the cashier picks how freight is handled: To Pay (customer pays the courier) or Charged (we add it to the bill, with its own GST line)
  • How the customer gets the order — pick one: Store Pickup (customer collects from the shop), Door Delivery (we ship to them), Godown Delivery (handed over at the warehouse), or DACC — Despatch Against Cash Collection (courier collects the balance on delivery)
  • Customer attached — search elAccounts for an existing customer (by name, phone or GSTIN), or add a new one without leaving the cart. Walk-in / no-customer is also valid for over-the-counter sales
  • Set due date for the payment, if the order is on credit — auto-derived from the customer's elBooks payment terms (e.g. Net 30), but the cashier can override
  • Change Warehouse — appears only when the store has more than one warehouse mapped to it; the cashier picks which warehouse fulfils this order
  • Checkout opens the payment window — the cashier picks the method and enters the amount
  • Payment methods supported — Cash, Card, UPI, Bank Transfer, Cheque (each method can be turned on or off per store)
  • Cash — system computes the change to return; non-cash — a reference is captured (UTR, cheque #, UPI ref or card slip) and an optional screenshot can be attached as proof for the Accounts team to review later in elBooks
  • Partial payment is allowed when the store setting permits it — the order can confirm with the balance still due; the rest is collected later via "Add Payment" from the order detail
  • On submit the cashier sees the receipt / proforma; it can be printed (compact or HSN-detailed layout) or sent to the customer over WhatsApp / Email
Orders
  • Orders list — every order ever made at the store, with search by order number, customer name or phone, payment method, or date range; status tabs and dispatch filters narrow the list further
  • Order status (the document state) — one of: Draft (cart saved, not committed), Pending Approval (committed but waiting for the elBooks accountant to approve the payment screenshot), Confirmed (committed; stock reserved; printable invoice), Voided — i.e. Cancelled (the order is annulled and any stock hold is released)
  • Payment status (the money fact, computed automatically from the payment history) — one of: Unpaid, Partial, Paid, Partially Refunded, Refunded
  • Despatch status (warehouse hand-off) — Pending, Ready for Despatch, or Dispatched; Delivery status — Pending or Delivered
  • Approval state (only visible when an approval workflow has touched the order) — None, Pending, Approved, Rejected, Cancelled
  • Order detail page shows everything for one order: the itemised receipt (each line with quantity, rate, tax breakdown — CGST / SGST / IGST), the payment history (every payment in / refund out, with the elBooks approval state per payment), customer details, billing & shipping address snapshot, the linked proforma number, and the final invoice number once stamped by elBooks
  • Actions from the order detail — Add Payment, Refund, Void (cancel the order) / Approve void (approve the cancellation), Approve / Reject the confirm request, Set Due Date, Request Payment, Send via WhatsApp / Email, Reprint, Download PDF, Download CSV, "Push to Dispatch" (for orders the warehouse hand-off missed), "Release Dispatch" (for held orders), Edit (while still editable)
  • Per-order CSV download — every order has a one-click CSV. It contains one row per line item with these columns: order number, order date, order status, payment status, customer name, customer phone, customer email, line number, item name, HSN code, quantity, UoM, unit price, line total, taxable value, CGST, SGST, IGST, total tax, order subtotal, order tax, freight mode, freight, surcharges, round-off, order total, amount paid, amount due. Same file works for accountant review, GST return reconciliation, or feeding into a third-party tool
  • Bulk Excel export by date range — a Download orders button at the top of the orders list opens a date-range picker; the system then exports an Excel .xlsx file with two sheets:
    • Orders sheet — one row per order with: Tracking code, Created at, Confirmed at, Status, Payment status, Customer name, Customer phone, Store ID, Terminal ID, Subtotal (₹), Discount total (₹), Surcharges (₹), Freight (₹), Tax (₹), Round-off (₹), Total (₹), First payment method, Cashier user ID
    • Order Items sheet — one row per line item with: Tracking code (matches the Orders sheet for VLOOKUP / JOIN), Line #, Item ID, Name, Vertical, Quantity, UOM, Unit price (₹), Discount (₹), Surcharge (₹), Taxable value (₹), HSN code, SAC code, GST rate %, Tax amount (₹), Line total (₹), Tax inclusive
    Filename is stamped orders_YYYY-MM-DD_to_YYYY-MM-DD.xlsx. The current status-tab filter is also honoured, so the cashier can download only Draft / Confirmed / Voided (cancelled) when needed

Movement of stock over any period — opening, in, out, closing
New

The reconciliation report — verifies that opening + in − out = closing for any window. Period-end stocktake matches against this report; any mismatch with physical count surfaces inventory leakage, recording errors, or theft.

The Show Data Corrections toggle separates real physical movement from manual adjustments — so the operations manager can see what was actual activity versus what was a recording fix. The flexible date presets remove the need for spreadsheet exports for routine windows.

3 · Inventory Turnover Report Product
How fast each item is moving — and how much capital it is tying up
New
Fields Shown
  • Item — produce or graded item
  • Opening — stock at start of period
  • Closing — stock at end of period
  • Avg Qty — (Opening + Closing) ÷ 2
  • Sold — total quantity sold in the period
  • Turnover — Sold ÷ Avg Qty (how many times stock turned over)
  • Days on Hand — average number of days a unit sits in stock
Filters & Options
  • Date range selector (same presets as Stock Summary)
  • Top N — show only the top-N or bottom-N items
  • Sort by — turnover, days on hand, or sold qty
  • Export to CSV
Business Insight

Separates movers from parkers. Items with high turnover and low days-on-hand are healthy — keep buying them. Items with low turnover and high days-on-hand are holding capital and warehouse space — buy less or reprice them.

Top N + Sort By turns the report into a focused list — "show me my worst 10 movers this quarter" is a one-click answer. The numbers replace gut-feel decisions on what to stock.

4 · Sampling Report Custom
Compares sampling-stage estimates against actual grading outcomes
New
Sampling Summary — Fields
  • Item — bulk produce sampled
  • Batch — batch identifier
  • Warehouse — sampling location
  • Sampler — staff member who took the sample
  • Sample Qty — quantity sampled
  • Purchase Qty — total purchased quantity for that batch
  • Bulk Cost / Unit — cost per unit of bulk produce
  • Conversion — projected conversion factor
  • Notes — sampler observations
Variations — Fields
  • Graded Item — output grade
  • Expected % — % projected from sampling
  • Expected Qty — quantity projected from sampling
  • Actual Qty — quantity actually produced
  • Actual % — % actually produced
  • Variation Qty — Actual − Expected (quantity)
  • Variation % — Actual − Expected (percentage points)
Visualisation
  • Sampling vs Actual — grouped bar chart comparing Sampling % and Actual % per graded item
Business Insight

Sampling is the basis for batch pricing — if sampling overestimates the premium grade, the batch is purchased at the wrong price. This report makes that risk visible: every batch's expected vs actual grade split is shown side-by-side, and the Variation columns quantify the gap.

The Sampling vs Actual graph surfaces systematic patterns — which sampler or which season produces optimistic estimates — so management can correct the sampling process upstream rather than absorbing the variance every batch.

Revised structure: Initially scoped as a single flat sample log. Restructured after review with Geogi into Sampling Summary + Variations + Sampling-vs-Actual graph to make the estimate-vs-outcome comparison directly actionable.
5 · Grading and Sorting Report Custom
Yield, loss, and cost per grade for every grading run
New
Fields Shown
  • Conversion — the grading conversion rule applied
  • Source Item — the bulk / ungraded input
  • Source Qty — input quantity that entered the run
  • Actual Output — total graded output produced
  • Yield % — Actual Output ÷ Source Qty
  • Loss — quantity lost to rejection, wastage, moisture
  • Cost / Unit — effective cost per unit of graded output
  • Grade Split — output distribution shown two ways: conversion-wise (as per the conversion rule) and distribution-wise (actual share per grade)
Business Insight

Every grading run converts cheap bulk into priced graded output. This report shows whether that conversion is profitable — Yield % and Loss tell you how much of the input made it through, and Cost / Unit tells you the true landed cost of each grade.

The dual Grade Split view — conversion-wise vs distribution-wise — lets management compare what the conversion rule predicted with how the output actually distributed across grades. A consistent gap means the conversion rule itself needs revisiting.

6 · Despatch Analysis Report Custom
Time from despatch creation to shipment — operational SLA monitoring
New
Aggregate Metrics
  • Average — mean time from despatch IN to shipment
  • Median — middle value, less skewed by outliers
  • P90 — the 90th-percentile time — the long tail
  • Sample Size — number of despatches in the period
Visualisation
  • Distribution histogram — count of despatches across time-to-ship bands
  • X-axis bands: < 4h · 4–24h · 1–2 days · 2–3 days · 3–7 days · > 7 days
  • Y-axis: number of despatches in each band
Business Insight

Fulfilment speed is what customers feel. Average alone hides the long tail — the P90 and the > 7 days band are the despatches that hurt the brand. This report quantifies them.

The histogram shows whether the despatch process is consistently fast (most despatches in the < 4h or 4–24h bands) or whether a non-trivial share is dragging into days. Median and P90 together tell management the typical experience and the worst experience — so improvement targets can be set against real numbers, not anecdotes.

📝 Discussion Note — June 3, 2026
Domestic Freight Charges — Management Interface
We need an interface to manage domestic freight charges. Calculating by weight × distance is the accurate approach, but we have a limitation: courier providers do not expose any API. The current process is:
  • Sales agents set the customer's preferred couriers.
  • Some couriers do not deliver to certain locations, so the courier is confirmed and set on request.
  • Before despatch, Jibin checks with the courier to find the per-kg rate.
  • Freight charges are then calculated based on that per-kg rate.
Proposed Approach
  • Provide a couriers interface with an option to update the per-kg rate for each courier.
  • For domestic orders, freight charges are auto-calculated based on the per-kg rate of the selected courier.
  • For international orders, the approach still needs to be worked out — to be discussed with Nibin.
  • Like the market price update, the courier per-kg rates must be kept current — a task system with notification should ensure the rates are updated every 15 days.
